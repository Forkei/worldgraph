# Research 13 — How expert investigators think: cognitive strategies across eight communities

*Research agent report, 2026-08-30. Sources verified by direct fetch of primary texts (doctrine PDFs, archive.org full texts, standards bodies, practitioner sites). Question: how do humans who are good at this actually work — strategies, not tools — and what does that imply for an engine.*

## 1. Skip tracers and private investigators
- **Inventory first, then funnel.** Every manual opens with what you already hold: name, DOB, last address, phones, employer, family ([Lyons, *Skip Tracing Manual*](https://archive.org/details/SkipTracingManual2018)). Klein: "start broad and narrow and funnel." Becnel: shrink a finite denominator of candidates — "the more we know about James, the better our odds."
- **One hard identifier unlocks the next.** Next of kin → Facebook; occupation → suburb; landline → home address. Klein: "that little piece of information that's going to get you to the next phase. Something solid you can hold onto."
- **Work rings outward.** Ex-partner, roommate, family, neighbours, **landlord**; "at least one old address usually had a family member living there"; "if she's not married, what's the boyfriend's name? Maybe the utilities are in his name." The subject's absence from records is routed around through the people whose records they appear in.
- **Verify by loop-closure against something the informant can't fake.** Lyons' "red fence" test (Street View a claimed address, ask about a neighbouring detail). A *location investigation* is complete only with physical confirmation.
- **Database "associates" are leads, not facts.** Chapter headings "Confirmation or Lead?", "Identify misinformation". Address history is read as a **timeline** ("timeline profiling").
- **Desk before phone** — know as much as possible before calling so lies are detectable. **Record everything; stop on budget or physical confirmation.**

## 2. Investigative journalists
- **The hypothesis is the method** (Hunter, [*Story-Based Inquiry*](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_vD7D9KcEa1wC)): "A hypothesis is a story and a method for testing it." Open sources before human sources: "it is always easier to get someone to confirm something you already know."
- **Minimum / maximum / worst-case story, pre-committed** — a *kill criterion* stated before work begins.
- **Disprove; change the hypothesis, never the facts.** "If the facts say the hypothesis is wrong, we change the hypothesis." When overwhelmed, "stop digging and look at the story your facts are telling."
- **"How do you know that?" — then "How else?"** (Buttry, [Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-1/verification-fundamentals-rules-to-live-by/2-verification-fundamentals-rules-to-live-by)). Verify source and content separately; "start from the assumption that the content is inaccurate."
- **Chronology as instrument**: a chronology spreadsheet including all contacts with sources; "go back in time" when searching.
- **Pivot on shared identifiers**: cross-reference website contact details with corporate filings; reverse-image accounts sharing a photo; ICIJ pivoted on a nominee director "named as a director of thousands of companies" and confirmed by cross-referencing two leaks; each reporter first "searched home."
- **What's missing**: "Does it feel as if something is missing? Who might present a different picture?" ProPublica methodology notes state what the data *cannot* show. **Data as a witness**: "explore the extreme values; compare a sample record against reality." Bellingcat: "Record and archive what you are doing. Provide feedback to yourself to correct your track and avoid rabbit holes."

## 3. Intelligence analysts
- **The cycle starts with a question, and the question may be wrong** ([JDP 2-00](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/311572/20110830_jdp2_00_ed3_with_change1.pdf)). PIRs decompose into *indicators* — "any positive or negative evidence"; "individual indicators cannot stand alone" ([FM 2-0](https://www.bits.de/NRANEU/others/amd-us-archive/fm2-0%2804%29.pdf)). Collection is driven by gaps: "an inventory of the data is the quickest way to see gaps."
- **ACH** ([Heuer](https://www.ialeia.org/docs/Psychology_of_Intelligence_Analysis.pdf)): "For each hypothesis ask: if this is true, what should I expect to be seeing or not seeing?" "Proceed by trying to reject hypotheses rather than confirm them. The most likely hypothesis is usually the one with the least evidence against it." Evidence weighed by *diagnosticity*; "unproven hypotheses should be kept alive until they can be disproved."
- **Absence of evidence is evidence**: "the steps the adversary has *not* taken may be more significant than the observable steps."
- **Assumptions surfaced early** (Key Assumptions Check); sensitivity to the few load-bearing items.
- **Two-axis source grading** (Admiralty): reliability A–F independent of credibility 1–6, with "the requirement to review and re-evaluate previously evaluated sources."
- **Against reflexive collection and satisficing**: "The reaction to many problems is to collect more information, even though analysts already have more than they can digest"; "pay particular attention to any feeling of surprise when new information does not fit."

## 4. Detectives and criminal investigators
- **ABC**: "Assume nothing; Believe nothing; Challenge everything" ([Core Investigative Doctrine](https://library.college.police.uk/docs/acpo/Core-Investigative-Doctrine.pdf)). "Every account should be checked for inconsistency or conflict with other material."
- **Gap matrix → hypothesis → line of enquiry → action.** Who/What/When/Where/Why/How × Known / Not known / Conflicts / Consistencies. Hypotheses are *only* for gaps: "a specific objective based on the gap or the conflict in evidence"; assumptions "explicitly recorded." Fast-track actions = those likely to establish important facts or preserve evidence ([MIM](https://library.college.police.uk/docs/acpo/murder-investigation-manual-redacted.pdf)).
- **HOLMES/MIRSAP is a task graph**: every returned document is read for new lines; each Action refers to one line of enquiry; resulted actions are reviewed "raising further Actions where necessary"; nulls recorded: *"Where these records have been searched with a negative result, the words 'no trace' should be entered … to show the research has been carried out."*
- **TIE**: define the population, **set elimination criteria before enquiries**, keep eliminations provisional; elimination strength ranked (forensic > description > independent alibi > associate alibi > spouse alibi); "choose wider rather than narrower time parameters."
- **Slow time and the policy file**: step back from the rush; the SIO's decision log is the definitive record. For low-information cases, investigate *why* information is low.
- **Tunnel vision and tipping points**: verification bias named in doctrine; Canada's wrongful-conviction report recommends independent review before a suspect is fixed and a designated contrarian; Fahsing & Ask found "decisional tipping points" (naming a suspect, fixing main lines) after which hypothesis generation collapses; **accredited detectives vastly outperformed novices while merely *experienced* ones did not — "length of experience alone does not predict sound judgments."**

## 5. Genealogists — the most formalized evidence epistemology
- **Genealogical Proof Standard**: reasonably exhaustive research · complete citations · analysis and correlation · resolution of conflicting evidence · written conclusion ([BCG](https://bcgcertification.org/ethics-standards/)). Mills: "Proof is not a document. It's a body of evidence"; "contradictions almost always emerge when research is thorough."
- **Three independent axes**: source original/derivative/authored · information primary/secondary/indeterminable · evidence direct/indirect/**negative** ("conclusions drawn from the absence of a situation that should exist given the circumstances"). "Whether any piece of information is evidence depends upon the research question."
- **FAN principle**: "When those we study left no document, we find it in the records created by their Friends, Associates, and Neighbors." "Bits and shards that prove nothing by themselves can be immensely valuable as pointers to other records." "If we don't have enough data to test, we broaden."
- **Identity by correlation, independence of corroboration**: "each other source must be independently created from the first, and each independently from each other." Same-name conflicts resolved by converging attributes in a proof summary.
- **Research logs and nulls**: reports include "each source consulted, including those that produced negative results"; FamilySearch: "research logs show negative evidence — record what you did and *what you mean by 'nil'*."
- **Incremental planning; adversarial self-test; stopping rule**: "What we find, each time, should determine what we do next"; hypotheses are "an interim state"; play devil's advocate; done when all relevant sources addressed, ≥ 1 original record with primary information, and "the risk of new evidence overturning this conclusion" is low.

## 6. Due diligence / KYC / forensic accountants
- **Predication gate; baseline-then-anomaly**: "understand the 'usual' to spot the 'unusual'."
- **The story test** (Wolfsberg): "not a documentary exercise — consider whether the source of wealth appears legitimate and plausible; significant gaps and material inconsistencies present challenges"; corroborate milestones with sources *independent from the customer*. FCA poor practice: "always accept a customer's explanation at face value."
- **Follow control, not names**: "the person in whose name an account is opened is not necessarily the person who controls the funds"; investigators move "from a company to its relationships and back."
- **A name hit is not an identity**: discount matches on differences in secondary identifiers (DOB, incorporation date); weight by corroboration across outlets. Aleph: add DOB, aliases, addresses before matching.
- **Closed-system reconstruction** (IRS net-worth method): "there are only three things a subject can do with money — spend, deposit, hoard"; "establish a reliable beginning net worth"; exculpatory leads "must be pursued." Benford "can never prove or disprove" — it says *look harder*.

## 7. Historians and archival researchers
- **External before internal criticism** (Langlois & Seignobos; Bloch): provenance and transmission first, then content; "the rule of methodical doubt"; "witnesses in spite of themselves" are the best witnesses.
- **Sourcing / contextualization / corroboration** (Wineburg): experts read a document first as a *positioned act* (who, when, why) before its content.
- **Questions are engines** (Fischer): the Baconian fallacy (researching without hypotheses), the fallacy of the possible proof, the "furtive fallacy" (assuming causes are always hidden and sordid).
- **Reading silences** (Trouillot): silences enter at fact creation, assembly, retrieval, and retrospective significance. Ginzburg's evidential paradigm: identity inferred from involuntary marginal details forgers neglect.

## 8. Expertise and cognition
- Recognition-primed decisions: experts categorize, generate one candidate, simulate, take the first not rejected (Klein). Insight is "triggered by detecting a contradiction," an impasse, or "seeing a connection."
- Abduction (Peirce): "The surprising fact C is observed; if A were true, C would be a matter of course; hence there is reason to suspect A."
- **Early hypotheses steer search** (Elstein): "generate a limited number of hypotheses early and use them to guide collection; each predicts what additional findings ought to be present."
- **Experts search less, better** (White, Dumais & Teevan): different vocabulary, different resources, more success; the advantage is stored patterns, not effort.
- **Stopping rules**: representational stability — "when understanding is no longer changing as new information is acquired" (Browne et al.); information foraging — leave a patch when marginal gain drops (Pirolli & Card).
- **Errors**: confirmation bias; pseudodiagnosticity (looking only at P(D|H)); **premature closure was "the single most common cause" of diagnostic error** (Graber); need-for-closure "seize and freeze" — investigators noticed inconsistencies only when an alternative perpetrator was made salient (Ask & Granhag); contextual bias flipped fingerprint experts' own prior identifications (Dror).

## 9. The common core, ranked by universality (n of 8 communities stating it explicitly)
1. **Every fact is a lead: "what does this now let me find?" (8/8).** Identifier chain; "what we find should determine what we do next"; every resulted action read for new actions.
2. **Corroborate across *independent* records; a name is never an identity (8/8).** "J. Smith" in a lawsuit is your J. Smith only if DOB, address period, and an associate line up.
3. **Doubt is the default; grade source and content separately, before reading content (8/8).**
4. **Hypotheses steer collection; each asked "if true, what would I see (and not see)?"; kept alive until disproved (7/8).** "He moved in with his mother" predicts a registration at her address — go check.
5. **Absence of an expected record is a finding, and nulls are logged (7/8).** A company claiming 200 employees with no payroll filings and no LinkedIn footprint.
6. **Timelines expose contradictions (7/8).** "Sold the business in 2015" but signatory on its 2018 filing.
7. **Work rings outward through the cluster (6/8).** Nothing on the subject after 2019 → search the spouse, co-director, landlord.
8. **Start from the hardest fact you have, not the most interesting (6/8).** Begin with the DOB on a court record, not the rumour about offshore accounts.
9. **Decompose into discrete, prioritized actions; keep a decision log with reasoning (6/8).** "Action #47: search deed index 2010–2020 for surname X; result: no trace; raised by action #12."
10. **Actively seek disconfirmation; institutionalize a contrarian; watch for tipping points (6/8).** Before promoting a merge, spend one action trying to split them.
11. **Anomalies are the highest-value attention signal (5/8).** A director on thousands of companies is not noise — it is the pivot.
12. **Stop when the loop closes, the picture stops changing, or the marginal source stops adding — and pre-commit to the criterion (5/8).** Budget is a ceiling, not the criterion.

## 10. What this means for an automated investigation engine
- **Facts as leads → a lead-derivation function on every new attribute**, typed by attribute kind. The primary generator of actions is deterministic; the LLM *suggests* non-obvious unlocks and never executes without a cost/diagnosticity score.
- **Identity by correlation → resolution as a proof object, never a string match.** ≥ 2 independent corroborating attributes; provenance independence checked (two databases copying the same upstream = one source); a "split test" action before any merge. LLMs confidently merge same-name entities — use them only to propose correlation features.
- **Source grading → two-axis provenance on every edge**, plus original/derivative; re-grade as corroboration accumulates. LLMs classify document *type* well; they must not assign reliability to sources with no track record.
- **Hypotheses steer collection → first-class hypothesis objects**: statement, the gap it addresses, predicted records if true / if false, status (unproven / disproved — never "proven"), assumptions. Plans derive from *diagnostic* predictions. **LLMs are genuinely good at generating the full competing-hypothesis set and expected-record lists** (where even experts fall short — Fahsing); poor at scoring consistency honestly, so make the matrix explicit and have a second pass challenge it.
- **Absence as finding → expected-record checklists per entity type × jurisdiction, and a research log with nulls** `{source, query, date, result: no trace, meaning}`. "Not found" is negative evidence only when the record was expected; otherwise it's a coverage gap. Never let the LLM infer absence from its own failure to think of something.
- **Timelines → a rule-based temporal consistency checker**; wide time parameters by default. LLM extracts dates and narrates; rules check.
- **Rings outward → ring-based expansion with a budget per ring and a reason per hop** (ring 0 subject; 1 household/officers; 2 relatives/co-directors/agents; 3 neighbours/associates), triggered by *silence in the inner ring*, not curiosity. Without this the graph grows indiscriminately — the novice pattern.
- **Start from the hardest fact → anchor scoring**; track which conclusions depend on which few items so a retracted anchor cascades.
- **Action graph + decision log → HOLMES-style actions**: line of enquiry, priority, raising action, result, spawned actions; an "office manager" pass reviews and files. The audit trail *is* the anti-tunnel-vision device.
- **Disconfirmation → an adversarial pass and a merge/promotion gate**: a devil's-advocate pass by a separate prompt/model that sees only the evidence, not the prior conclusion (Dror's context isolation); log the tipping point. LLMs seize-and-freeze on their own earlier outputs.
- **Anomaly detection → baseline-then-deviation and structural flags**; LLMs notice "something's off" in narrative but misjudge base rates — pair with statistics.
- **Stopping rules → explicit termination per question**: representational stability (graph around the goal unchanged for N actions) + GPS checklist (≥ 2 independent sources, ≥ 1 original, conflicts resolved, expected-record list exhausted) + foraging threshold + pre-committed kill criterion. Budget is a ceiling.

**The LLM must not:** assert identity matches; decide a record doesn't exist; assign source reliability from priors; score its own hypotheses; decide it has done enough; invent leads without a cost estimate. **It is genuinely good at:** enumerating competing hypotheses and expected observations; extracting entities/dates/roles; proposing unlock queries from a new fact; drafting proof arguments; contextualizing documents; flagging narrative inconsistencies for rule-based checkers to verify.
